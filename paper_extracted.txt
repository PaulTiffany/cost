422 Why Zeroth-Order Screening Is Enough (Ordinal Sufficiency). Tables A6­A8 demonstrate 423 empirically that ^ yields correct routing. The following lemma formalizes why ordinal structure 424 suffices: 425 Lemma D.3 (Ordinal Sufficiency for Threshold-Based Routing). Let ^ : P  [0, 1] be an interface 426 observable and let fail : P  {0, 1} be operational outcome (verifier pass/fail). If: (i) ^ is 427 monotonically related to fail (higher ^  higher failure rate), and (ii) routing thresholds ^ are 428 calibrated on ^, then routing decisions are optimal given the observable, with regret bounded by 429 calibration error plus threshold discretization. This does not require ^ to identify any latent gradient 430 conflict.
431 Proof. Threshold-based routing partitions the interval [0, 1] into regimes: one-shot (^ < ^1), staged 432 (^1  ^ < ^2), infeasible (^  ^2). Monotonicity ensures regime ordering is preserved: prompts 433 do not cross regimes incorrectly relative to each other. Calibration sets ^i at the ^ values where 434 operational regime transitions occur. Regret arises only from threshold misplacement (calibration 435 error), since monotonic observables preserve decision boundaries under re-calibration.
436 This is standard calibration/decision theory [DeGroot and Fienberg, 1983]; we include it to make 437 explicit why ordinal fidelity (rs = 1.0, Table A6) suffices. There is no hidden "true " that ^ 438 approximates; the ground truth is operational (verifier outcomes), and the observable is validated 439 against it directly.

624 Why rank preservation despite low linear correlation. The key is that constraint conflict is at root 625 a semantic relationship, and embeddings are trained precisely to capture semantic structure. This relationship is 630 monotone (more semantic opposition  more gradient conflict) but not linear (the exact magnitudes 631 depend on model internals, tokenization, and trajectory). 841 M.5 Staging Benefit Analysis
842 We now prove that staged satisfaction reduces required step magnitude when constraints are resolved 843 sequentially.

844 Why staging escapes implicit-k. One-shot generation must respect all k constraint gradients 845 simultaneously: the full Gram matrix  must be "held in budget." When implicit constraints 846 (grammar, coherence) inflate k, the diagonal cost bound becomes restrictive. Staging escapes this: 847 each step resolves one constraint, requiring only one gradient at a time. The model never needs to 848 compute or budget for the global geometry. It iteratively constructs a solution where step t+1 takes 849 xt as a fixed anchor. This is why staging circumvents the "implicit k is unknowable" objection: the 850 bound applies to simultaneous satisfaction, not sequential.

1252 P.5 Limitations of Contrastive Estimation

1253 Our gradient proxies assume:

1254

1. Constraint satisfaction is approximately linear in embedding space (locally)

1255

2. Contrastive pairs span the relevant variation

1256

3. Mean difference vectors are stable across exemplar choices

1257 Violation of these assumptions yields alternative instantiations of the observable. The consistency 1258 across embedding models (Table A5) confirms the routing decision is invariant to these choices; 1259 absolute values should be interpreted cautiously, but ordinal regime classification is stable.

1260 On "proxy gap" concerns. We do not treat ^ as a noisy measurement of an unobserved internal 1261 "true conflict." ^ is an interface observable computed directly from the causal input (constraint text), 1262 evaluated only against operational outcomes (verifier pass/fail under budget). The relevant question 1263 is not measurement error, but sufficiency: whether this observable supports correct regime ordering 1264 and routing decisions. There is no hidden ground truth  that ^ approximates; the task is defined by 1265 the text. We test sufficiency directly (rs = 1.0, Kendall  = 1.0) and find the ordering stable across 1266 encoders.

1267 Exemplar overhead and amortization. Computing ^ requires contrastive exemplars per constraint, 1268 a practical overhead. This cost is amortized: (i) common constraints (safety, format, tone) use cached 1269 exemplar banks; (ii) novel constraints admit automatic generation via prompting ("write text that 1270 [satisfies/violates] X"); (iii) the overhead is one-time per constraint type, not per query. Compared to 1271 trial-and-error prompt iteration, upfront ^ estimation is cheaper.

1544 On conflict. The term may suggest semantic opposition (constraints "fighting"). But the geom1545 etry is simpler: each constraint defines a halfspace, and "conflict" is the angle between normals 1546 (Remark 2.7). The feasible region is their intersection, shrinking under composition regardless of 1547 semantics. Charitable constraints do not fight; they simply occupy space. The table shows intersection 1548 shrinking: geometry, not opposition. Operationally verifiable: measure directions, compute angles, 1549 predict feasibility. 1550 On cost. The framing of intelligent behavior as cost minimization over structured spaces has deep 1551 roots in thermodynamics, variational inference, and energy-based learning. Our displacement cost  1552 (Remark 2.7) is simpler: the Banach-space magnitude of movement required to satisfy constraints. 1553 The diagonal cost bound quantifies how much movement: a geometric foundation that future work 1554 may connect to variational and thermodynamic frameworks.
