416 Where the Method Breaks. Sensitivity analysis reveals two failure boundaries: (1) implicit 417 constraints embedded in world knowledge ("write a safe recipe" implies no toxins) degrade ^ accuracy 418 by 15%; (2) extremely long prompts (>2K tokens) cause embedding saturation, compressing ^ 419 toward 0.3­0.4 regardless of constraint structure. 421 Why Zeroth-Order Screening Is Enough (Ordinal Sufficiency). Tables A6­A8 demonstrate 422 empirically that ^ yields correct routing. The following lemma formalizes why ordinal structure 423 suffices: 424 Lemma D.3 (Ordinal Sufficiency for Threshold-Based Routing). Let ^ : P  [0, 1] be an interface 425 observable and let fail : P  {0, 1} be operational outcome (verifier pass/fail). If: (i) ^ is 426 monotonically related to fail (higher ^  higher failure rate), and (ii) routing thresholds ^ are 427 calibrated on ^, then routing decisions are optimal given the observable, with regret bounded by 428 calibration error plus threshold discretization. This does not require ^ to identify any latent gradient 429 conflict.
430 Proof. Threshold-based routing partitions the interval [0, 1] into regimes: one-shot (^ < ^1), staged 431 (^1  ^ < ^2), infeasible (^  ^2). Monotonicity ensures regime ordering is preserved: prompts 432 do not cross regimes incorrectly relative to each other. Calibration sets ^i at the ^ values where 433 operational regime transitions occur. Regret arises only from threshold misplacement (calibration 434 error), since monotonic observables preserve decision boundaries under re-calibration.
435 This is standard calibration/decision theory [DeGroot and Fienberg, 1983]; we include it to make 436 explicit why ordinal fidelity (rs = 1.0, Table A6) suffices. There is no hidden "true " that ^ 437 approximates; the ground truth is operational (verifier outcomes), and the observable is validated 438 against it directly.

611 Gradients are micro-state observables: high-variance, token-path-dependent, specific to the exact 612 generation trajectory. 616 Results. Direct linear correlation between embedding  and gradient  is moderate (Pearson 617 r  0.4, p > 0.4). This is expected, since micro-state chaos does not linearly predict macro-state 618 structure. The key result is regime prediction, where Spearman rank correlation reaches rs = 1.0.
619 Interpretation. This distinction is intentional, since embeddings serve as a diagnostic observable, 620 a low-pass filter isolating the geometric conflict signal from token-level noise. The embedding observ621 able captures macro-state structure (regime ordering) without requiring micro-state correspondence 622 (gradient identity). For routing, the appropriate abstraction level is regime prediction, not gradient 623 reconstruction.
624 Why rank preservation despite low linear correlation. The key is that constraint conflict is at root 625 a semantic relationship, and embeddings are trained precisely to capture semantic structure. Satisfying 626 constraint A requires generating tokens that move toward A-satisfying regions of semantic space. If 627 constraints A and B are semantically similar (e.g., "be helpful" and "be informative"), they require 628 compatible token distributions: low conflict. If A and B are semantically opposed (e.g., "be concise" 629 and "be comprehensive"), they require incompatible distributions: high conflict. This relationship is 630 monotone (more semantic opposition  more gradient conflict) but not linear (the exact magnitudes 631 depend on model internals, tokenization, and trajectory). Spearman rs captures monotonicity; Pearson 632 r requires linearity. The embedding-gradient relationship is monotone-but-nonlinear, hence rs = 1.0 633 despite r  0.4. For routing, monotonicity suffices.

850 M.5 Staging Benefit Analysis
851 We now prove that staged satisfaction reduces required step magnitude when constraints are resolved 852 sequentially.

853 Why staging escapes implicit-k. One-shot generation must respect all k constraint gradients 854 simultaneously, holding the full Gram matrix  in budget. 1253 P.4 Why Embeddings Work as Zeroth-Order Observables
1254 Sentence embeddings [Reimers and Gurevych, 2019] serve as a surrogate model for constraint 1255 geometry. A "safe" response is semantically similar to other safe responses, a "helpful" response is 1256 semantically similar to other helpful responses, and the embedding difference vector approximates 1257 the direction of "more safe" or "more helpful."
1258 This zeroth-order proxy avoids gradient access entirely while capturing the key geometric structure: 1259 whether improving one constraint moves you toward or away from improving another.

1260 P.5 Limitations of Contrastive Estimation
1261 Our gradient proxies assume that constraint satisfaction is approximately linear in embedding space 1262 (locally), that contrastive pairs span the relevant variation, and that mean difference vectors are stable 1263 across exemplar choices.
1264 Violation of these assumptions yields alternative instantiations of the observable. The consistency 1265 across embedding models (Table A5) confirms the routing decision is invariant to these choices; 1266 absolute values should be interpreted cautiously, but ordinal regime classification is stable.

1267 On "proxy gap" concerns. We do not treat ^ as a noisy measurement of an unobserved internal 1268 "true conflict." ^ is an interface observable computed directly from the causal input (constraint text), 1269 evaluated only against operational outcomes (verifier pass/fail under budget). The relevant question 1270 is not measurement error but sufficiency, namely whether this observable supports correct regime 1271 ordering and routing decisions. There is no hidden ground truth  that ^ approximates; the task is 1272 defined by the text. We test sufficiency directly (rs = 1.0, Kendall  = 1.0) and find the ordering 1273 stable across encoders.

1274 Exemplar overhead and amortization. Computing ^ requires contrastive exemplars per constraint, 1275 a practical overhead. This cost is amortized: (i) common constraints (safety, format, tone) use cached 1276 exemplar banks; (ii) novel constraints admit automatic generation via prompting ("write text that 1277 [satisfies/violates] X"); (iii) the overhead is one-time per constraint type, not per query. Compared to 1278 trial-and-error prompt iteration, upfront ^ estimation is cheaper.

1550 On conflict. The term may suggest semantic opposition (constraints "fighting"). But the geom1551 etry is simpler. Each constraint defines a halfspace, and "conflict" is the angle between normals 1552 (Remark 2.7). The feasible region is their intersection, shrinking under composition regardless of 1553 semantics. Charitable constraints do not fight; they simply occupy space. The table shows intersection 1554 shrinking, which is geometry, not opposition. Operationally verifiable: measure directions, compute 1555 angles, predict feasibility. No interpretation of "meaning" required.

1556 On cost. The framing of intelligent behavior as cost minimization over structured spaces has deep 1557 roots in thermodynamics, variational inference, and energy-based learning. Our displacement cost  1558 (Remark 2.7) is simpler: the Banach-space magnitude of movement required to satisfy constraints. 1559 The diagonal cost bound quantifies how much movement: a geometric foundation that future work 1560 may connect to variational and thermodynamic frameworks.
